A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Mandeville Restoration Co provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Mandeville, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared

Common Sewage Cleanup Questions

A sewage backup is an extreme health hazard. The viruses, parasites and bacteria in raw sewage can lead to E. coli infection, respiratory disease, hepatitis and other serious illness. Without the right protective gear, nobody should attempt the cleanup. For professional cleanup, reach our team at (601) 205-7767 without delay.
In most cases our advice is to relocate temporarily while the cleanup happens, above all when contamination is widespread. Both the decontamination chemicals and pathogens in the air can be a risk to your health. We will advise you based on the specific situation in your home.
First, get everyone, animals included, away from the contaminated rooms. Stop using sinks, toilets and appliances that drain, since more water makes the backup worse. Switching off the heating and cooling and the affected circuits is wise, provided you stay clear of the contaminated water while doing it. Leave the cleanup alone, and wash well with soap if any got on your skin.
Some things can be kept and others cannot. Hard, non-porous items such as metal, glass, sealed tile and solid plastic can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Anything porous the sewage touched must go, which covers mattresses, upholstered furniture, drywall, insulation, carpet and padding. For insurance purposes, our crew keeps a list of everything that had to be removed.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Typical culprits are tree roots entering the pipe, grease hardening inside it and wipes or similar items flushed down a toilet. Older lines can also crack or collapse, and heavy rain can overload the municipal sewer and push sewage back through floor drains. To know for certain, have the line camera-inspected once cleanup is finished.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
